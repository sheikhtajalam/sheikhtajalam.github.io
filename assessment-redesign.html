Academic Assessment System Redesign

Redesigning assessment frequency to create a more continuous student-feedback cycle.

01 · Objective

What needed to change

Move from four formal examinations toward more continuous assessment and earlier identification of learning gaps.

02 · My ownership

Where I was accountable

Assessment calendar, guidelines, teacher coordination, stakeholder communication, monitoring and adoption support.

03 · Outcome

What changed

Six formal examinations plus mandatory biweekly tests were implemented; all subject teachers adopted the test cycle and test attendance reached 100%.

The challenge

The existing assessment structure used four formal examinations, with students and parents tending to place greater emphasis on the Half-yearly and Annual examinations. This could leave learning gaps unnoticed until relatively late in the session.

The objective was to create more continuous assessment and feedback.

The change

4 formal exams
Longer intervals
Limited continuous monitoring
Learning gaps identified later
6 formal exams
Biweekly tests
Continuous records
Improvement actions

My role

I redesigned the assessment calendar, assigned dates, coordinated teachers, communicated the changes, created guidelines, monitored implementation, reviewed schedules and addressed complaints and concerns. All 22 teachers were involved in implementation.

Stakeholder resistance

Teachers initially raised concerns about increased workload. I addressed those concerns through consultation and individual conversations, connecting the change to student growth and earlier identification of improvement areas.

I was also newly appointed and had to establish trust within a new institutional and cultural environment. Addressing concerns individually helped build acceptance.

Implementation & monitoring

The redesigned system introduced six formal examinations alongside mandatory biweekly class tests. Student records were consistently updated, and implementation was reviewed through ongoing coordination and Saturday staff meetings.

Delegation to relevant academic and examination in-charges allowed the process to operate without centralizing every task.

Results

All subject teachers began conducting biweekly tests. Teachers became more active and strategic in session and test planning. A notable behavioural outcome was 100% attendance for the tests after some students had previously avoided or skipped them.

Parent connectivity also improved through PTMs, home visits, WhatsApp class groups and record books, with parents beginning to provide suggestions and feedback.

What this demonstrates

Change management · Stakeholder management · Process improvement · Scheduling · Communication · Delegation · Monitoring
